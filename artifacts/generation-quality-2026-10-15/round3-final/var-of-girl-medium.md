Strengths

Lily engaged with the trial lesson readily and demonstrated a willingness to participate from the very first exchange. Her vocabulary for occupations was a clear highlight — she produced well-formed, confident sentences describing what a doctor and a farmer do, showing that she has internalized more than just individual words. Her reading aloud was fluent and natural, and she showed a particularly impressive moment of creative engagement when she added her own thought — "He is brave" — unprompted after finishing the written text. This kind of spontaneous contribution suggests genuine enthusiasm for language rather than passive compliance with tasks. Lily also responded to corrections during the lesson with reasonable receptiveness, and by the end of her exchange about the weekend, she had incorporated at least part of the feedback she was given.

Areas for Improvement

Past tense verb forms are the most immediate area for Lily to continue developing. Throughout the lesson, she consistently used present tense forms where past tense was required — "go" instead of "went," "eat" and "play" instead of "ate" and "played," and "help" instead of "helped." Even after corrections were modeled by the teacher, she carried the adjustment forward only partially. Regular, focused practice with simple past verbs — both regular and irregular — would help this pattern become more automatic for her.

Learning Suggestions

Future lessons would benefit from dedicating structured time to simple past tense, with particular attention to high-frequency irregular verbs such as "go/went" and "eat/ate." Activities that pair familiar topics — such as weekends or stories — with guided past-tense narration would give Lily meaningful context for practicing these forms, which seems to be the kind of engagement she responds to well.
