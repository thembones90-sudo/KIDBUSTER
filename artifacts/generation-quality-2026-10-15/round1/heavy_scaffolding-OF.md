Strengths

Ava approached her first trial lesson with a willingness to try, which is an encouraging foundation for a young learner. She showed she could repeat target vocabulary when given a clear model, producing "bread" and "I like bananas" after hearing them from the teacher. When prompted to complete a sentence pattern, she was able to follow along and finish with the key word, as she did when she said "banana" in response to the teacher's prompt. Ava also responded to a choice question by selecting "juice" and then successfully repeated the full sentence "I like juice" with the model provided. Her honest "I don't know" when she was unsure of a word shows a comfortable willingness to communicate rather than shut down, which is a positive sign for future learning.

Areas for Improvement

Ava would benefit from continued practice with basic sentence structure, particularly the use of articles such as "an" — during the lesson she produced "It's apple" rather than "It's an apple," and this is an area that more repetition can help reinforce over time. She may gain confidence through regular exposure to the same vocabulary across multiple lessons, as most of the food words introduced today required teacher modeling before Ava could attempt them. Matching and recognition activities could also help build her ability to connect words to images more independently with practice.

Learning Suggestions

Future lessons would benefit from revisiting the same core vocabulary — food words, simple "I like" sentences, and basic articles — across several sessions to give Ava the repeated exposure she needs to begin retaining them. Short, visual matching activities paired with plenty of modeled sentences would be a well-suited format for her current stage.
