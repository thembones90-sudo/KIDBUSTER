Strengths

Kai engaged with the trial lesson in a friendly and willing manner, greeting the teacher and responding to prompts throughout the session. He showed a basic familiarity with animal vocabulary, correctly identifying an elephant and a giraffe without hesitation. When introduced to simple sentence structures, he made genuine attempts to produce them — for example, moving from "It's a elephant big" to the correctly ordered "It's a big elephant" after hearing the model. He also offered a spontaneous observation about the giraffe's neck, showing some willingness to use English beyond one-word answers. Toward the end of the lesson, he produced "The monkey can climb" with only light support, which was one of his stronger moments. He responded well to repetition and correction, and with a clear model to follow, he was generally able to reproduce the target phrase. His participation was cheerful and his overall attitude toward the lesson was open.

Areas for Improvement

Kai would benefit from more practice placing adjectives in their correct position in English sentences, as he tended toward a home-language word order in his first attempts. He could also continue developing his use of modal verbs — phrases like "can" and "can't" required repeated modeling before he produced them reliably. Staying focused throughout the lesson is another area where Kai could grow, as occasional off-topic comments suggest he may need support maintaining attention during structured activities.

Learning Suggestions

Future lessons would be well spent reinforcing basic sentence structure — particularly adjective placement and simple modal constructions — through short, repetitive activities that keep engagement high. Animal topics and visual flashcards seemed to capture Kai's interest and could serve as a useful vehicle for introducing new vocabulary and patterns in a way that suits his learning style.
