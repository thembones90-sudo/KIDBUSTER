This kind of engagement — choosing to take part rather than hold back entirely — is a positive sign in a first lesson environment, which can feel unfamiliar and a little daunting for younger learners. Areas for Improvement

Ben was notably quiet for much of the lesson and relied on the structure of the group activity — particularly the song — to engage, rather than volunteering responses or contributions independently. Building his confidence in speaking up and responding during more open moments of a lesson would be a helpful area of focus going forward. With continued exposure to a supportive lesson environment, he is likely to become more at ease and willing to participate across a wider range of activities.

Learning Suggestions

Future lessons would benefit from continuing to use song, rhythm, and visual support as entry points, since these formats clearly helped Ben feel comfortable enough to join in. Gradually introducing simple, low-stakes speaking opportunities — such as naming pictured items or completing short phrases with support — would help build the confidence and verbal participation that Ben can continue to develop over time.
