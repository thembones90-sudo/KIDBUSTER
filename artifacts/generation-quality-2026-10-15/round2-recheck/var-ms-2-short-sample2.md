Strengths

Lily came into the trial lesson ready to engage and responded to questions with genuine effort throughout. Her recall of job-related vocabulary was a clear highlight — she described what a doctor and a farmer do using full, well-formed sentences, showing a comfortable grasp of the lesson's core topic. Her reading of the short firefighter passage was fluent and confident, and she added her own unprompted comment — "He is brave" — which showed real initiative and a willingness to go beyond the text in front of her. Lily's listening comprehension was reliable throughout; she followed instructions and transitions between activities without difficulty. She also responded well to correction, incorporating the teacher's model fairly quickly, as when she adjusted "go" to "went" after hearing the corrected form.

Areas for Improvement

Past tense verb forms were a consistent area of challenge during the lesson. Lily used present tense forms where past tense was needed — for example "eat" and "play" instead of "ate" and "played" — and even after hearing the correction, full uptake was not always consistent within the same response. Possessive forms also came up briefly, with "grandma's" requiring a prompt. Continued, focused practice on irregular and regular past tense verbs in context would help these forms become more automatic over time.

Learning Suggestions

Future lessons would benefit from dedicating regular time to past tense structures, particularly irregular verbs and regular "-ed" endings, using short storytelling or personal narrative activities where Lily can practice these forms in a natural, low-pressure setting. Weaving in possessive 's alongside vocabulary work would also support the patterns that surfaced during this lesson.
